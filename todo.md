# To do

Ideas for making the Mythos Notebook cooler and more useful. The first three build on the two-page spread.

## Best fit for the book

1. **Compare mode.** Show two cultures' versions of the same myth side by side, one per page, such as
   Utnapishtim on the left and Noah on the right. Lining up parallel tellings is what comparative mythology
   is about, and the spread now makes it possible. Pick the pair from the concept page, or press a key to
   compare with the next culture.
2. **Use the empty right pages.** Short tellings leave the right page mostly blank. That space could hold:
   - the picture at full size;
   - a small map showing where the culture is;
   - "where this motif goes next": the closest parallels from other cultures, each with a line on whether
     they likely share an origin, were borrowed, or arose independently. The caveats and summaries already
     hold this information.
3. **A map on each concept page.** Pins show every culture that tells the myth, so you can see at a glance
   that the flood story is everywhere while the stolen fire clusters in a few regions. Click a pin to open
   that telling. The map can be a self-contained inline drawing, so the site still needs no build step.

## Making it more useful

4. **Motif relationships.** A diagram per concept showing which tellings descend from a common ancestor
   (for example Atrahasis → Gilgamesh → Noah), which travelled with trade or conquest, and which are
   separate inventions. This makes the "similar doesn't mean related" point visible.
5. **Reading aids.**
   - Bookmarks and a "continue where you left off" option.
   - A "random myth" button.
   - Marking tellings already read.
   - Making the site installable and readable offline.
6. **Timeline.** Each source placed by date, such as Atrahasis around 1700 BCE, Hesiod, the Popol Vuh and
   the Eddas. It would show the order in which a motif was first written down.

## Content

7. **Fill the thin traditions.** Hittite has only 8 stories, and Andean and West African have 13 each.
   Whole traditions are missing, such as Mongolian and Turkic, Bantu, Inuit and Ainu. Write new entries
   following `tools/CONTENT_BRIEF.md` and check them with the build. Some traditions need care about sacred
   or restricted material, such as Aboriginal Australian stories.

**Suggested order:** #1 and #2 first. Both use the spread, and together they'd make the book feel like a
real comparative edition rather than a set of separate pages.
